ThirdParty/stb_vorbis/stb_vorbis.c
  stb_vorbis v1.22 by Sean Barrett, http://nothings.org/stb_vorbis/
  Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nothings/stb/master/stb_vorbis.c (2026-11-11)
  SHA-256 as downloaded: 4c7cb2ff1f7011e9d67950446b7eb9ca044f2e464d76bfbb0b84dd2e23e65636
  SHA-256 with the fixes: 7ac2402c7713a8d87c826bae2f99f77c8da1923a7290a62385f3e0e06d7ab0ab
  Used for Ogg Vorbis (Source/Helix/Audio/Decoding/Implementation/VorbisDecoder.cpp), always with an alloc buffer.
  Fixed in our copy:
  - decode_residue: residue type 2 limited its range to n * 2 values instead of n * channels, so files of more than
    two channels that use it (ffmpeg's libvorbis 5.1) decoded to noise. Found by the 5.1 decode test.
  - start_decoder: vendor and comment lengths read from the header were not checked, so a length of 0xFFFFFFFF
    made len + 1 zero and the terminating write landed outside. Lengths above 16 MB and more than 2^20 comments
    are now an invalid setup. Found by the Vorbis fuzz target.
  - vorbis_deinit: after a setup that failed part way, the residue tables held garbage pointers that the cleanup
    walked. With an alloc buffer every free does nothing, so the cleanup now returns at once in that case. Found by
    the Vorbis fuzz target.
  - make_block_array, decode_residue, inverse_mdct: when the alloc buffer had no room left, temp_alloc gave NULL
    and the pointer table was written through it (a residue of a damaged setup can ask for any amount of
    classification memory). A full buffer is now VORBIS_outofmem for that packet. Found by the Corpus / Audio
    fuzz runs under AddressSanitizer (round 2394233 of helix_audio_fuzz).
